Reasons I Love You

Your soul, because I love the part of you no eyes can see.

Your mind, because it never ceases to amaze me.

Your heart, because there is so much kindness in you.

Your light, because you make even ordinary days feel brighter.

Your dreams, because I want to see every one of them come true.

Your presence, because everything feels better when you are near.

Your strength, because you keep moving forward and inspire me to do the same.

Simply you, because after all these years, I would still choose you.

A letter for you

The One Who Taught Me to Look Toward Tomorrow

There is one thing I have never thanked you for enough.

You did not simply make my life happier.

You changed the way I looked at it.

There were moments when the world felt terribly heavy, when tomorrow seemed like nothing more than another distant day waiting to arrive.

And then there was you.

Your voice.
Your presence.
Your laughter.

The simple knowledge that somewhere in this enormous world, there was someone I loved and who loved me in return.

You reminded me that tomorrow could hold something beautiful.

That there could still be things I had not seen.
Places I had not visited.
Dreams I had not dreamed yet.
Little moments that had not happened.

You gave me another reason to look forward.

Not because you were meant to carry the weight of my life upon your shoulders.

You never had to save me by becoming my entire world.

You simply became one of the brightest reasons I wanted to keep discovering what the world could still become.

And I will always be grateful for that.

Perhaps that is why I think of you whenever I imagine the Japanese idea of musubi—the invisible thread that brings people, moments, and destinies together.

I do not know whether some invisible thread truly led me to you.
But sometimes I look at our story and wonder how else I could explain it.

Out of all the lives being lived at the same time as ours,
out of all the places we could have been,
out of all the people we could have passed without ever knowing,
somehow our paths met.

And somehow, they kept meeting.

Again.
And again.

Until you became part of my life so deeply that imagining it without you feels like trying to imagine the sky without the moon.

Perhaps that is what our thread is.

Not a chain.
Not a promise that nothing will ever change.

But a quiet connection that says:

“No matter how much we grow, I will remember where I found you.”

You became a light I could recognize.

I think there is something beautiful about tsukimi, the simple act of looking at the moon.

The moon does not ask anything of us.
It does not need to be touched to be beautiful.
We simply look at it, and somehow the night feels less empty.

Sometimes I think you have been like that for me.

Not because you erased every difficult thing.
Not because you made the darkness disappear.

But because your presence gave me something beautiful to look toward.

And sometimes, that is enough to make a night feel different.

You have reminded me that life is not made only of its hardest moments.

It is also made of laughter in the middle of an ordinary day.
Of conversations that last far longer than they were supposed to.
Of dreams whispered between two people.
Of silly jokes.
Of plans for the future.
Of discovering something new and immediately wanting to tell the other person.
Of sitting beside someone you love and realizing that, for that moment, you are exactly where you want to be.

You taught me to notice those things.
To treasure them.
To want more of them.

And perhaps that is one of the greatest gifts you have ever given me.

You helped me remember that there is beauty in simply being here to witness it.

If our hearts have scars...

Then perhaps I understand kintsugi a little differently now.

There is something beautiful in the idea that what has been broken does not have to be hidden.

The cracks become part of the story.
They become part of what makes the object itself.

I think people are like that too.

We grow.
We change.
We carry memories.
We make mistakes.
We sometimes become tired.

And none of that makes us less worthy of love.

You do not have to be untouched by life for me to admire you.
I do not want some flawless version of you.
I want the real you.

The one who has lived.
The one who is still learning.
The one who sometimes doubts himself and still keeps moving.
The one who has dreams that are still waiting to become reality.
The one whose soul I have had the privilege of knowing little by little, year after year.

Every part of you belongs to the person I love.

The polished parts.
The uncertain parts.
The beautiful parts.
The unfinished parts.

All of them.

And perhaps that is why I love you so deeply.

There is a Japanese expression, mono no aware, that carries the feeling of being moved by the beauty of things precisely because they are temporary.

Perhaps love feels a little like that.

We know that moments pass.
Childhood passes.
Years pass.
People change.
Even the happiest days eventually become memories.

And yet, knowing that does not make them less precious.
It makes me want to hold them more carefully.

So I want to remember you.
Not only as you are now.
But in every version of you I have been lucky enough to know.

The boy you once were.
The person you are today.
The man you are still becoming.

I want to remember your laughter.
Your dreams.
The things that make your eyes light up.
The moments when you are proud of yourself.
The moments when you need someone to remind you that you are capable of more than you think.

I want to remember all of it.

Because one day, years from now, I want to look back and think:

“I was there.”

I was there when you were still becoming yourself.
I was there when you were learning.
I was there when you succeeded.
I was there when things were difficult.
I was there when we were young and still had so much of life ahead of us.

And most importantly—

I was there to love you.

If I were given another lifetime...

I do not know whether souls truly find one another again.

But if they do,
I hope mine remembers yours.

I hope that somewhere, in some distant place, beneath some unfamiliar sky, I would hear your voice and feel that strange certainty again.

That quiet feeling of:

There you are.

And if I had to cross another thousand roads to find you,
I think I would.

Not because I believe there is only one person in the entire universe capable of making me happy.
But because, after knowing you, I know what it feels like to find someone whose soul feels familiar.

Someone whose existence makes the world feel a little warmer.
Someone who inspires me to become more.
Someone I can admire without putting on a pedestal.
Someone I can love without needing perfection.
Someone I can choose freely.

And I would choose you freely.

Every time.

So thank you.

Thank you for every ordinary day you made feel special.
Thank you for every laugh.
Every conversation.
Every dream.
Every little moment that reminded me there was still so much life waiting ahead.

Thank you for being a place where my heart could rest.
Thank you for inspiring me.
For believing in me.
For making me want to keep learning, creating, dreaming, and becoming.

Thank you for letting me know your soul.

Because of all the things I could have found in this enormous world,
somehow,
I found you.

And the world was kind enough to let me keep you beside me.

I will never consider that an ordinary thing.
I will treasure it.

Like moonlight on a quiet night.
Like a repaired piece of porcelain with gold running through its cracks.
Like a thread connecting two lives that somehow found one another.

And if life gives us many more years,
I want to fill them with memories.

Not perfect ones.
Real ones.
The kind that become precious precisely because they belonged to us.

So when you look toward the future, remember this:

There is someone who believes in the person you are becoming.
Someone who admires the soul behind your eyes.
Someone who is endlessly grateful that your path crossed hers.
Someone who has found, beside you, even more reasons to look toward tomorrow.

And when the years have passed,
when we have changed in ways we cannot yet imagine,
I hope I can still look at you beneath some quiet moon and recognize the same soul.

The same feeling.
The same you.

And I hope I can smile and say:

“Of all the lives I could have lived,
I am grateful that mine led me to you.”

Still you.
Always you.

And, if I am allowed to choose,
you again.
